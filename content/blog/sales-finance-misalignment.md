Sales-finance misalignment is the gap between how sales and finance define, measure and time the same results. Sales sees bookings, attainment and the commissions it expects to earn; finance sees billings, revenue, cash and commission expense. When the two sets of numbers are built from different targets, measures, data and calendars, every quarter ends in a reconciliation exercise instead of a decision, and the incentive plan rewards outcomes finance did not plan for.

This guide covers where the gap opens, the six root causes, the symptoms that point to each, a sequence for closing it, and where platforms and agents help. It is not accounting or legal advice: confirm the accounting treatment of commissions and any plan terms with your finance and audit advisers.

## How misalignment shows up in attainment

The first visible symptom is usually attainment that does not match the plan: quota distributions that make no sense to finance, or forecasts built on quotas that never summed to the financial plan. The mechanisms that connect SPM to attainment are covered in [how SPM affects quota attainment](/blog/how-spm-affects-quota-attainment), and the planning side, including quota methods, in [the complete guide to SPM alignment for quota accuracy](/resources/guides/spm-alignment-quota-accuracy). This guide starts one step earlier, at the seams between the sales and finance functions.

## Targets: quotas and the financial plan diverge

Finance builds the operating plan; sales operations turns part of it into quotas. The two drift apart when quotas are set on a different version of the plan, when over-assignment above the financial target is decided without finance, or when quota relief granted mid-year is never reflected in the forecast. Sales then reports attainment against one total while finance tracks performance against another, and both are right on their own terms.

The fix is a single target hierarchy with an explicit, agreed bridge between the financial plan and the sum of quotas, including any deliberate over-assignment, and a rule that every quota change updates both sides.

## Measures: bookings, billings, revenue or cash

Sales plans commonly pay on bookings or contract value because sellers control them and they arrive quickly. Finance plans and reports on billings, recognized revenue or cash. A multi-year deal, a deferred start date or a large discount can look excellent in bookings and modest in revenue for the year, so a plan can pay accelerators on results finance will not see until later, or never.

None of the measures is wrong. The misalignment comes from choosing one for the plan without stating how it relates to the measure finance plans on, and without a true-up when the two diverge, for example on cancellations, credits or non-payment.

## Incentive logic outside the comp budget

Plan features that motivate sellers can also change cost in ways nobody modeled: uncapped accelerators, decelerators removed after a difficult quarter, SPIFs added mid-year, overlay and team splits that credit the same deal several times, or recoverable and non-recoverable draws extended to new hires. When these are designed by sales leadership and costed afterwards, the comp budget becomes a forecast of the plan finance approved, not of the plan in force.

Each plan component should be modeled for cost at several attainment levels before approval, and every mid-year addition should come with its own cost estimate and an owner on the finance side.

## Data flows: CRM, SPM platform and ERP disagree

Crediting usually comes from the CRM, calculation runs in the SPM platform, and actuals, invoices and commission accruals live in the ERP. Each system can be correct on its own and still disagree with the others: an opportunity edited after close, an account reassigned mid-period, an invoice raised in a different month from the booking, or a credit note the CRM never sees.

Without a reconciliation between the three, accrual estimates drift and period close stalls on manual tie-outs. The controls and ownership model for this data are covered in the [incentive compensation data governance guide](/resources/guides/incentive-compensation-data-governance), and the integration patterns in [Enterprise SPM for CRM and ERP integration](/solutions/enterprise-crm-erp-integration).

## Timing: close calendars, accruals and retroactive changes

Sales thinks in quarters and payout dates; finance thinks in close calendars and accrual periods. When the commission calculation finishes after the books close, finance has to accrue from an estimate and true up later. When a territory, quota or plan change is applied retroactively, earlier periods are recalculated after they were reported, and commission expense moves between periods in ways nobody forecast.

Aligning the two calendars means agreeing the cutoff for data that counts in a period, when the calculation must finish relative to close, how estimates are booked, and how retroactive changes are dated and approved.

## Governance: decision rights and exception approval

Many of the gaps above persist because nobody owns them. Sales leaders approve exceptions to win a deal, finance discovers them in the accrual, and the plan document says nothing about who may grant what. Global companies add regional variants that drift from the central plan without anyone deciding they should.

Clear governance assigns decision rights for plan design, plan changes, crediting disputes and exceptions; sets approval thresholds; and records every decision with its approver and date. How this works across countries is covered in the [global SPM operating model guide](/resources/guides/global-spm-operating-model).

## Symptoms and their likely causes

| Symptom | Most likely root cause |
| --- | --- |
| Quotas do not sum to the financial plan, or the bridge between them is unexplained | Targets |
| Strong attainment and payouts in a quarter that finance reports as weak | Measures |
| Commission expense runs over budget at normal attainment | Incentive logic outside the comp budget |
| Close stalls on manual tie-outs between commission results and the ledger | Data flows |
| Large true-ups to commission accruals after close | Timing |
| Exceptions discovered by finance after they were promised to sellers | Governance |

Most organizations find more than one cause behind a single symptom, so treat the table as a starting point for investigation rather than a diagnosis.

## A sequence to fix it

1. **Agree the measures.** For each role, document the measure the quota is set in, the measure the plan pays on, the measure finance plans on, and the true-up rule that connects them.
2. **Set decision rights.** Name who owns plan intent, budget, accrual treatment, plan changes and exceptions, with approval thresholds.
3. **Bridge the targets.** Build one target hierarchy from the financial plan to the sum of quotas, with over-assignment stated explicitly.
4. **Cost the plan.** Model each plan component at several attainment levels before approval, and require a cost estimate for any mid-year addition.
5. **Reconcile the systems.** Define how CRM, SPM platform and ERP are reconciled each cycle, with tolerances and an owner for every break.
6. **Align the calendars.** Agree data cutoffs, the calculation deadline relative to close, how estimates are booked, and how retroactive changes are dated.

Measures and decision rights come first because every later step depends on them. Reconciling systems that encode different measures only documents the disagreement.

## Where platforms and agents fit

An SPM platform gives sales and finance one calculation, one audit trail and one set of results to reconcile against the ERP. It enforces the measures, crediting rules, approvals and effective dates the two teams have agreed, and it keeps the history that explains why a payout or an accrual changed.

Agentic SPM adds a layer on top of that governed data: agents can validate inbound loads before calculation, compare results against the ledger, flag accrual variances and route exceptions to the right approver, logging every action for audit. A person still approves anything that affects pay. Neither the platform nor the agents can decide what the plan should measure, which is why the design work comes first.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is sales-finance misalignment?

Sales-finance misalignment is the gap between how sales and finance define, measure and time the same results. It shows up when quotas do not sum to the financial plan, when the comp plan pays on a different measure than finance reports, when the CRM, SPM platform and ERP disagree, or when changes are approved by one side without the other.

### What causes sales and finance to disagree on numbers?

Usually one of six things: targets set separately, different measures such as bookings against revenue or cash, incentive logic designed outside the compensation budget, data flows between CRM, SPM platform and ERP that are not reconciled, close and accrual calendars that do not line up, and unclear decision rights over plan changes and exceptions.

### Should commissions be paid on bookings or revenue?

It depends on what the plan is meant to reward and when the company considers a sale final. Many companies credit on bookings for timely motivation and true up against invoices, revenue or cash. Whichever basis you choose, the plan document must state it, finance must be able to reconcile it, and the accounting treatment should be confirmed with your finance and audit advisers.

### Who should own the sales compensation plan, sales or finance?

Neither alone. A workable split gives sales leadership ownership of plan intent and role design, finance ownership of the budget, cost modeling and accrual treatment, and a joint committee the decision rights over plan changes and exceptions above an agreed threshold, with every decision recorded.

### How do SPM platforms help align sales and finance?

An SPM platform gives both teams one calculation, one audit trail and one set of results to reconcile against the ERP. It enforces whatever measures, crediting rules and approvals the two teams have agreed, but it does not decide them, so the alignment work comes before the configuration.
