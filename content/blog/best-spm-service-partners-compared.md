The best sales performance management consulting service for 2026 is the one that publishes depth on the job you are actually hiring for. Enterprises buy SPM services for five different reasons, and few firms are deep in more than three: redesigning a compensation plan that has drifted from strategy, integrating CRM and ERP data so incentive analytics can be trusted, governing incentives across countries, getting a complex organization ready for a platform implementation, and keeping the whole thing running after go-live. This comparison sorts ten independent firms by what they publish on each of those jobs, then by platform.

## The five jobs, and why they are not the same purchase

![The five jobs enterprises hire SPM partners for: plan redesign, integration and analytics, global governance, implementation readiness, and managed services after go-live](/images/spm-consulting-five-jobs.svg)

**Compensation plan redesign.** The visible deliverable is a new plan document. The real deliverable is a cost model: what the proposed measures, rates, accelerators, and crediting rules would have paid against last year's actuals, by role and by territory. A firm that cannot show you that model is selling a point of view. Redesign also has to survive contact with the platform, which is why the firms that pair design with configuration tend to produce plans that calculate cleanly.

**Analytics and CRM and ERP integration.** Most incentive accuracy problems are data problems. The order came from the ERP with the wrong ship-to, the opportunity owner changed in the CRM after the credit date, the HR feed moved someone's role mid-quarter. Sales compensation analytics is only as good as the mapping from those source fields to the calculation, and the reconciliation from the calculation back to finance. The firms that describe mapping and validation as separate work, done before configuration, are the ones treating integration seriously.

**Global incentive governance.** Who approves a plan in each country, how local employment rules shape what can be paid and when, how currency is handled at calculation and at statement, and what evidence an auditor can pull without a consultant in the room. Offices in several countries are not the same as a published governance model, and the firms here mostly publish offices or delivery models rather than a governance model.

**Enterprise implementation readiness.** Plan documentation, data cleanup, decision rights, and process design done before the build starts, so the build is configuration rather than discovery. Several firms publish a readiness assessment as a named service, which is a good sign: it means they have seen enough implementations stall to productize the fix.

**Managed services after go-live.** The fifth job is the one that runs longest and gets scoped least. It comes in two forms that are often sold under one name. Platform support keeps the system healthy: production fixes, plan and quota changes, vendor releases, integration monitoring. Comp operations runs the cycle itself: data validation, calculation runs, statements, dispute investigation, and accrual inputs for Finance. The firms worth shortlisting publish which of the two they sell, and at least one of an SLA, coverage hours, or a price. The [seven questions to vet an enterprise SPM partner](/blog/7-questions-to-vet-an-enterprise-spm-partner) covers selection across all five.

## How we compared

- **Inclusion rule.** A firm is listed if its own website publishes SPM or ICM platform implementation, consulting, or post-implementation support. Design-only consultancies and vendor-owned services teams are not listed, for reasons below.
- **Evidence rule.** Every claim about a firm comes from that firm's own pages, each listed in the sources with the date we retrieved it. Where a firm does not publish something, the table says "Not published". No guessed pricing, no ratings, no analyst quotes.
- **Order.** Alphabetical. Nothing here is a ranking.
- **Not listed.** The Alexander Group and Korn Ferry publish sales compensation design and benchmarking but not platform implementation or support; they belong on a design-only shortlist. The large systems integrators are left out by choice: their SPM work sits inside broader platform alliance practices, and this comparison covers firms whose SPM services stand on their own. Vendor professional services teams are not listed because they are the vendor.

Lanshore is one of the ten firms. Its entry is held to the same evidence rule and uses only what Lanshore publishes.

## 1. Ackle Consulting Group

Ackle describes its business in one sentence: it designs how revenue teams are paid, governs the data behind the numbers, and builds the AI software that runs it. That is a wider claim than most firms its size make, and the sales compensation page backs it with specifics. Plan design comes with modeling and cost-of-plan analysis, and the firm says each element is modeled against last year's actuals before anyone signs. Implementation is described as configure, integrate across CRM, ERP, and HRIS, test against real payout history, and train the admins. ICM platforms named are Xactly, Varicent, SAP SuccessFactors, CaptivateIQ, and Oracle. After go-live, a retainer covers monthly close, plan changes, disputes, new-hire setup, and executive reporting. The firm was founded in 2015 and says it serves teams of 50 to 5,000 sellers.

**Strongest on.** Plan redesign tied directly to configuration and administration, for mid-size sales organizations.

**Ask about.** Client references at your payee count, and where the team is based; the site does not say, and no case results are published.

## 2. Blue Horizons Group

Blue Horizons, based in Louisville, Kentucky, publishes four service lines: advisory, implementation, enablement, and managed services. Advisory covers go-to-market strategy, incentive compensation plan design, solution selection, and SPM readiness assessments. Implementation is described as end-to-end, run on a proprietary methodology the firm says cuts project timelines, across eight elements that include requirements alignment and source system integration. Managed services are on-demand operational and technical support tailored to the client's comp calendar and plan changes. Its partners page links implementation-partner pages for Varicent, Xactly, and CaptivateIQ, and a client testimonial describes design, build, and installation of Varicent incentive compensation software.

**Strongest on.** Readiness assessment and a structured implementation for Varicent, Xactly, and CaptivateIQ customers.

**Ask about.** How many of its implementations were on your platform, and what the eight implementation elements look like in a plan.

## 3. Canidium

Canidium organizes its services into strategic focus, operational alignment, technology for scale, and platform enablement, and it is one of the few firms here that names readiness as a product twice: annual sales plan readiness for the yearly plan cycle, and implementation readiness before a build. It also publishes a current and future state assessment and roadmap, compensation plan design, and data management and reporting. SAP, Xactly, and Varicent are its SPM software partners, alongside CPQ and pricing platforms. The site cites four global offices and has published guidance on where multinational compensation rollouts stall. Managed services are described as maintaining and optimizing software solutions with proactive issue prevention, plus technical health checks and staff augmentation.

**Strongest on.** Readiness and roadmap work for SAP and Xactly customers that also have CPQ or pricing in scope.

**Ask about.** SPM-specific managed service scope, since the managed services page covers all of Canidium's practices.

## 4. Delbridge Solutions

Delbridge sits at the junction of SPM and corporate performance management, and its SPM page reads that way: design, manage, and optimize incentive compensation; territory and quota planning; unified analytics and reporting; and integration with CRM, HCM, and ERP, delivered from model design and data integration through workflow configuration and enablement. SPM platforms named are Xactly, Latitude39, Anaplan, and InfinitySPM, beside a long CPM list that includes OneStream, Workday Adaptive Planning, and Planful. Its managed services page is one of the most specific in this group: administration, plan and quota and territory changes, commission run support, integration monitoring, release management, performance tuning, and documented plan logic, with stated SLAs and escalation paths. Offices are in Toronto, Bucharest, Wilmington, Pune, Costa Rica, and Valencia.

**Strongest on.** Analytics and integration for companies that want SPM and FP&A on connected platforms, with a written support scope afterward.

**Ask about.** Varicent, SAP, and CaptivateIQ, which do not appear on the SPM partner list, and how the six offices divide delivery.

## 5. IncentiveOps

IncentiveOps is the only firm here that publishes prices, and its implementation page is unusually explicit about method. Every engagement follows Assessment, Build, Launch. Plan logic is documented in operational detail before any system is configured, and that document becomes the build specification. Data integration is scoped and validated before configuration begins. Platform selection is a separate deliverable with a documented rationale that Finance and Sales leadership can both evaluate. Implementation projects are listed at $30,000 to $60,000 depending on plan volume, system complexity, data environment, and timeline. Its Fractional Ownership service then runs the full cycle, including validation, calculation, statements, disputes, and accruals aligned to Finance's close, at a published $12,000 to $18,000 per month, for companies with 30 or more quota-carrying sellers and no dedicated comp owner.

**Strongest on.** Redesign and implementation for scaling B2B companies that want the comp function run afterward.

**Ask about.** Multi-country programs and large payee counts, which the pages do not describe, and which ICM platforms the team has configured.

## 6. Lanshore

Lanshore's consulting page starts from auditability: finance and audit need to trace any payout back to its source transactions, the rule applied, and any adjustment. Its engagements begin with a plan design review that maps each plan component to its data source and calculation rule and, where the design should change, proposes options and models their cost before anything is configured. Requirements and data mapping across the CRM, ERP, HRIS, and spreadsheets that feed each plan comes next. In the telecom example on that page, the review found that roughly 6 percent of the prior year's new-logo bonus dollars were overpayments. On [global programs](/solutions/global-compliance), Lanshore cites a PepsiCo rollout across 20-plus countries and a dozen currencies, and on [CRM and ERP integration](/solutions/enterprise-crm-erp-integration) it cites more than 20 distinct source systems integrated across its engagements. It implements nine platforms: Varicent, Xactly, CaptivateIQ, SAP SuccessFactors Incentive Management, Anaplan, Salesforce Spiff, Performio, Akeron, and Incentivate. After go-live, Lanshore runs comp operations as a [managed service](/solutions/spm-managed-services) for a predictable monthly fee, with [SPM Operations](/agentic-spm/operations) agents doing data loads, calculation runs, validations, and exception queues, and every agent action logged with timestamp, input, output, and approver where applicable. For a national telecom carrier, Lanshore reports that after it implemented the platform and put agents on the monthly cycle steps, cycle close time dropped 74 percent against the pre-implementation baseline. It also reports a crediting-check agent at a national telecom carrier that cut the crediting exceptions the comp team works by hand from roughly 400 a month to around 100.

**Strongest on.** Audit-ready redesign and integration, multi-country rollout, and one partner from design through agent-run operations.

**Ask about.** Approval checkpoints and an audit-trail export for agent-run steps, which are newer than traditional outsourcing; Lanshore does not publish a support SLA or coverage hours.

## 7. OpenSymmetry

OpenSymmetry has the widest published service catalog in the group. Strategy services include SPM program assessment and future state planning, sales planning strategy, incentive compensation plan design support (with plan modeling as a named step), SPM technology evaluation and selection, talent strategy, and channel incentives. Solution deployment covers implementation, testing, and training, on an approach the firm describes as AI-enabled. Managed services include SPM Operations, with monitoring and functional support 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, 365 days a year, proactive monitoring of data loads and batch jobs, and dual-shore options from offices in Austin, London, and Pune. It lists eleven supported technologies, including Varicent, Xactly, SAP, Anaplan, CaptivateIQ, NICE, Oracle, and Spiff.

**Strongest on.** Breadth: assessment through selection, implementation, and round-the-clock support across the most platforms of any firm here.

**Ask about.** Integration and analytics as distinct services, which the pages do not break out, and whether the AI-enabled approach reaches operations or stops at implementation.

## 8. Spaulding Ridge

Spaulding Ridge frames SPM as part of a planning cycle that connects sales planning, forecasting, and incentives to the financial picture, with each phase informing the next. The SPM page covers territory and quota planning, capacity planning, incentive compensation aligned with organizational goals, and sales crediting with forward- and backward-looking transparency that it says reduces disputes. Analytics draws on the enterprise data landscape and includes forecast-to-actuals and weekly snapshot variance modeling. Named technologies are Anaplan, OneStream, Oracle NetSuite, Salesforce, and Snowflake. Managed services are cloud application managed services with a dedicated customer success team and a single point of contact.

**Strongest on.** Incentives and crediting tied to enterprise finance planning, for companies already on Anaplan or Salesforce.

**Ask about.** Who supports the incentive calculation if it runs on a dedicated ICM engine rather than Anaplan, and whether SPM managed service scope includes commission cycles.

## 9. SpectrumTek

SpectrumTek positions itself as going beyond ICM into the rest of SPM, and its consulting pages are structured that way: plan design, territory and quota planning, and a software selection service built around a scoring framework for evaluating vendors. The plan design page centers on proactively modeling payouts per group against forecasted performance, with what-if analysis, and on making sure plans work with IT systems and business processes before implementation. Technology partners are CaptivateIQ, Jedox, Oracle, SAP, Varicent, and Xactly. Managed services provide end-to-end support to enhance and maintain SPM systems, including on-demand admin support, data loads, batch processing, and job monitoring on a dual-shore model that the firm says delivers cost savings and round-the-clock support, tuned to SLAs. The footer lists an India office.

**Strongest on.** Vendor selection, plan modeling, and dual-shore round-the-clock support across six partner platforms.

**Ask about.** Multi-country governance and release management, which are not described, and whether admin support can extend to owning the cycle.

## 10. WorkCentive

WorkCentive, based in Toronto, publishes three things with unusual directness: implementations with data integration built in, strategic reporting and analytics tailored to daily business operations, and advisory from plan optimization to revenue strategy. Its managed services pick up post-go-live opportunities and changes with familiar consultants experienced in the client's model, plus training and health checks, including a save-or-scrap review for a troubled build. The about page shows Varicent, Performio, and CaptivateIQ as supported partners.

**Strongest on.** Continuity, and an outside opinion on whether an existing build is worth saving.

**Ask about.** Plan modeling and global programs, which are not published, and coverage hours.

## How to choose

![Decision path: start with the job, then filter by platform, then confirm handover to managed services](/images/spm-consulting-how-to-choose.svg)

Start with the job, not the platform. If the plan itself is wrong, you need a firm that publishes cost modeling and will show you the model. If the plan is right and the numbers are not, you need integration and analytics work, and the firm should be able to describe source-field mapping and reconciliation to finance without being prompted. If you run plans in many countries, ask for the governance model and a reference in a jurisdiction like yours. If you are about to implement, buy a readiness assessment before you buy the build. Then filter by platform using each firm's own list, ask how many consultants currently work on it, and get the handover to managed services written into the statement of work. Our [SPM consulting](/solutions/spm-consulting) page describes how Lanshore scopes the first three jobs, and the [platform comparison](/spm/compare) covers the platforms themselves.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best sales performance management consulting service for complex incentives?

The one that publishes how it models a plan change before configuring it, names your platform on its own partner list, and can show an audit trail from a payout back to the source transaction. On the pages we reviewed, Ackle, Lanshore, OpenSymmetry, and SpectrumTek publish plan design with modeling; the others describe plan work more briefly. Complexity is usually in crediting and data, not in the rate table, so weight integration experience heavily.

### Which sales performance management providers handle CRM and ERP integration well?

Judge by what they publish about the data, not the connector. Firms that describe mapping source fields, validating data before configuration, and reconciling results to finance are doing integration as an operational discipline. In this comparison, IncentiveOps and Lanshore describe integration in those terms; Ackle names CRM, ERP, and HRIS integration and testing against real payout history, and Delbridge names CRM, HCM, and ERP integration as a service; Blue Horizons and WorkCentive name it as an implementation element; the rest describe it in general.

### Which services help mid-market companies redesign compensation plans?

For mid-market teams the useful question is who will own the plan after the redesign. Ackle and IncentiveOps publish redesign tied to ongoing administration on a retainer or monthly fee, which suits a company without a comp owner. Lanshore and Blue Horizons publish redesign tied to platform implementation and readiness. If you already have a comp team and only need the design, The Alexander Group and Korn Ferry are design specialists that this comparison does not list because they do not publish platform services.

### How do companies modernize complex global sales performance management processes?

In waves, by country group, on one plan catalog and one data model, with governance decided before the first wave. The firms that publish multi-country delivery here are Lanshore (a 20-plus-country rollout on its global compliance page), OpenSymmetry (offices in Austin, London, and Pune with dual-shore delivery), SpectrumTek (dual-shore with an India office), Delbridge (offices in six countries), and Canidium (four global offices). Publishing offices is not the same as publishing governance, so ask for the approval model.

### Which sales performance management consulting services suit large enterprises?

Large enterprises need breadth of platform coverage, round-the-clock support, and evidence of multi-country programs. OpenSymmetry and SpectrumTek publish 24/7 or round-the-clock monitoring; OpenSymmetry, Lanshore, Ackle, and SpectrumTek publish five or more platforms; Spaulding Ridge publishes incentives tied to enterprise finance planning. Confirm consultant headcount on your specific platform, which no firm here publishes.

### How is SPM consulting different from SPM managed services?

Consulting changes the design: the plans, the crediting rules, the data flows, and the governance. Managed services keep that design running after go-live: fixes, plan changes, releases, and sometimes the commission cycle itself. The handover between the two is where scope gets lost, so ask which team owns what from the first cycle after go-live.

### What is an SPM managed service provider?

An SPM managed service provider keeps your sales performance management platform and process running after go-live. Depending on the contract, that means break-fix support, plan and quota changes, vendor release management, integration monitoring, and in some cases running the commission cycle itself, including calculations, statements, and dispute handling.

### What is the difference between SPM platform support and comp operations outsourcing?

Platform support keeps the system healthy: fixing production issues, applying plan changes, and managing upgrades while your team runs the cycle. Comp operations outsourcing hands over the cycle itself: data validation, calculation runs, statements, disputes, and accrual inputs for Finance. Several firms in this comparison offer both, so the contract should state which tasks the partner owns.
